= Mission National Parks: Explore, Scale, Verify
:navtitle: Mission National Parks
:page-toclevels: 2
:experimental:

Welcome to OpenShift 5. You are the platform engineer responsible for signing off a working three-tier National Parks application before the next phase of the workshop.

In this 25-minute mission you will use the *OpenShift console* to discover the application, trace its traffic path, scale the frontend, and check OpenShift Lightspeed's live findings against actual cluster evidence.

[cols="1,1,1,1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
| 0–2 min | 2–6 min | 6–11 min | 11–17 min | 17–22 min | 22–25 min
| Briefing | Explore | Trace | Scale | Challenge AI | Sign off
|===

[IMPORTANT]
.Mission rules
====
* Use only your assigned project, *`{lab_user}`*. Your console identity and terminal service account might differ; do not perform the exercises as a shared administrator.
* The application is already provisioned. Do not create a cluster, rebuild images, load the database, inspect Secrets, or change other namespaces.
* The screenshots currently show numbered *placeholders*. Your instructor will replace these with actual OpenShift 5 console captures. They are not examples of live cluster state.
* Keep the National Parks application running. Any scaling in this module must be reset to its original replica count before continuing.
====

== Mission 0: Check in (2 minutes)

Open {openshift_console_url}[the OpenShift console] and sign in as *`{lab_user}`* using the workshop credentials. Use the console project selector to choose *`{lab_user}`*.

The terminal might be logged in as a Showroom service account. If you are unsure, confirm its identity:

[source,bash,role="execute"]
----
oc whoami
----

Select your application project in the same terminal:

[source,bash,role="execute"]
----
oc project {lab_user}
----

*Checkpoint 0:* Both the console and terminal are scoped to your assigned application project. Ask the instructor to resolve missing permissions or unexpected identities before continuing.

== Mission 1: Meet National Parks (4 minutes)

Start with the *application*, not its YAML.

. In the console, open menu:Workloads[Topology] and locate the `national-parks-app` grouping in your project.
. Select the `parksmap` frontend, open its *Resources* panel, and follow its Route link.
. Move around the National Parks map and select a park marker. You should see real park data.
. Return to Topology and identify these three workloads:

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
| Workload | Purpose
| `parksmap` | Browser-facing map frontend
| `nationalparks` | API returning park data
| `mongodb-nationalparks` | Internal database
|===

image::module01-s01-map.png[Placeholder S01 - National Parks map, width=900]

image::module01-s02-topology.png[Placeholder S02 - National Parks topology, width=900]

*Predict before looking:* Which workloads need public Routes? Should MongoDB have one?

*Verify:* In the console, inspect the available Routes. The `parksmap` and `nationalparks` application Routes expose frontend and backend traffic; MongoDB should remain internal. Your instructor has initialized the park collection before the lab.

*Checkpoint 1:* You can open the populated map, identify all three components and explain why the database is not directly exposed.

[TIP]
====
An API health endpoint that returns `OK` does not necessarily confirm database access. Seeing nonempty park data validates more of the application path.
====

== Mission 2: Follow a request (5 minutes)

How does a browser request reach the right container?

*Working model:* `Route → Service → EndpointSlice → ready pod`

. In menu:Networking[Routes], select `parksmap`. Find its hostname, TLS configuration, and target Service.
. Before opening the Service, predict its name. Then go to menu:Networking[Services] and select `parksmap`.
. Read the Service selector and port. In menu:Workloads[Pods], find the frontend pod and check its labels and readiness.

image::module01-s03-route.png[Placeholder S03 - parksmap Route details, width=900]

image::module01-s04-service.png[Placeholder S04 - parksmap Service details, width=900]

The console shows the relationships, but a short API read can expose the current EndpointSlice addresses. Run:

[source,bash,role="execute"]
----
oc get endpointslices.discovery.k8s.io \
  -l kubernetes.io/service-name=parksmap \
  -o jsonpath='{range .items[*]}{range .endpoints[*]}{.addresses[0]} ready={.conditions.ready}{"\n"}{end}{end}'
----

*Expected:* At least one endpoint with `ready=true`. Record the number of *ready* addresses; you will compare it with the number after scaling.

*Checkpoint 2:* You can name the Route, its target Service, the Service selector and at least one ready endpoint.

[NOTE]
====
A Service can select multiple ready pods. This establishes eligible backends, but it does not prove which *individual* pod handled a specific browser request.
====

== Mission 3: Change capacity in the UI (6 minutes)

*Your prediction:* If you double the frontend replica count, will OpenShift create another Route, change the hostname, or add another ready Service endpoint?

. In *Topology*, select `parksmap` and open its *Details* panel. Locate the current replica control. If your console presents scaling in the Deployment page, open menu:Workloads[Deployments] → `parksmap` and use the available scaling action.
. Confirm the starting state is *1 desired / 1 ready*. Change the desired frontend replicas from *1 to 2* using the UI.
. Wait until *2 desired / 2 ready*. Watch the topology or pod list update.

image::module01-s05-scale-before.png[Placeholder S05 - frontend before scaling, width=900]

image::module01-s06-scale-after.png[Placeholder S06 - frontend after scaling, width=900]

. Refresh the **same** National Parks map URL. The existing Route should still serve a populated map.
. Re-run the exact EndpointSlice command from Mission 2. Count ready addresses again.

*Expected:* Two ready frontend pods and two corresponding ready Service endpoints, with the same Route hostname. A terminating or not-ready pod does not count as a ready endpoint.

*Checkpoint 3:* You personally performed the 1→2 scale operation in the OpenShift console and observed the expected change in ready backends.

[WARNING]
====
If the replica count immediately returns to 1, or if the second pod is not ready within roughly 90 seconds, **stop** and show the observed state to the instructor. A HorizontalPodAutoscaler or GitOps reconciliation rule might be affecting the result. Do not fight the controller or edit random resources.
====

== Mission 4: Challenge OpenShift Lightspeed (5 minutes)

You already know what the console shows. Can Lightspeed independently inspect that same live state?

. Open *OpenShift Lightspeed* using the chat icon in the lower-right corner of the console.
. Copy the following request. It is deliberately scoped to your own namespace and to read operations:

[source,text,subs="attributes+"]
----
Use live OpenShift MCP read tools in namespace {lab_user}.
Inspect Route parksmap, its target Service, that Service's
EndpointSlices, and the current ready frontend pods.

Report the Route hostname, Service name, ready endpoint
addresses, and pod names/UIDs. Identify successful tool calls
and their results. If live access is unavailable or denied,
say so explicitly. Do not read Secrets, exec into pods,
modify anything, or inspect another namespace.
----

. Inspect the returned resource details and, where available, actual tool call and tool result evidence. Merely saying “I used MCP” is not proof of a successful read.
. Verify at least one named resource and its current pod UID against this terminal output:

[source,bash,role="execute"]
----
oc get pods -l app=parksmap \
  -o custom-columns='NAME:.metadata.name,UID:.metadata.uid,PHASE:.status.phase'
----

image::module01-s07-lightspeed.png[Placeholder S07 - Lightspeed live-resource inspection, width=900]

*Checkpoint 4:* Choose the **honest** result of your investigation:

* *Verified live read:* A successful read/tool result is visible and a resource fact matches independent console/API evidence.
* *Supplied-data explanation:* The AI only interpreted text or output that you supplied.
* *Unavailable or inconsistent:* No successful live access was verified, or facts do not match.

[TIP]
====
If Lightspeed cannot access the live namespace, the lab still succeeds through your own console/API evidence. Do not assume that a fluent AI response proves cluster access. The scale operation itself is a console action, not an AI command.
====

== Mission 5: Sign off and reset (3 minutes)

. In Topology or menu:Workloads[Deployments], confirm the frontend and backend show healthy status. Look at recent logs or events for obvious errors. If useful, inspect the backend logs quickly:

[source,bash,role="execute"]
----
oc logs deployment/nationalparks --tail=10
----

image::module01-s08-health.png[Placeholder S08 - healthy workload and recent logs, width=900]

. Return to the `parksmap` scaling control and restore the frontend to *1* replica. Wait for *1 desired / 1 ready*, and verify the map still opens.
. Record your readiness card. This becomes the starting evidence for the remaining modules.

[cols="2,4",options="header"]
|===
| Evidence | Your result
| Project | `{lab_user}`
| Map and park data | Working / Needs attention
| Route → Service | `parksmap` → `parksmap`
| Final frontend replicas | 1 desired / 1 ready (confirm)
| Lightspeed evidence | Verified / Supplied-data only / Unavailable
|===

*Checkpoint 5:* The National Parks application remains functional and the scale exercise is reset.

== You've completed the mission

You now have a healthy baseline, a verified request path, hands-on scaling evidence and a documented AI result. This is the application you will manage through GitOps, resource controls, incident investigation and recovery.

== Troubleshooting quick reference

[cols="2,4",options="header"]
|===
| Symptom | What to do
| Wrong project or `Forbidden` | Confirm identity and `{lab_user}`; ask instructor to check student RoleBindings.
| Map missing markers | Tell instructor; pre-provisioned application/data may not be ready. Do not call `/ws/data/load`.
| No ready endpoints | Inspect frontend pod readiness and Service selector; stop the exercise if setup is unhealthy.
| Scale button missing or count reverts | Ask instructor to check `deployments/scale` RBAC, HPA and GitOps reconciliation.
| Lightspeed did not show a tool result | Mark live access unverified and use your own console/API observations.
| Terminal session lost | Re-run `oc project {lab_user}`; no hidden session variables are required.
|===
